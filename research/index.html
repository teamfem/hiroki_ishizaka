Research programmes and
mathematical architecture.

The age of AI is changing the scale of scientific problems that can be explored. I have chosen two principal research programmes in response: stable reduction of unresolved dynamics in large, multiscale systems, and mathematically certified numerical computation. They address distinct questions, but both seek to make increasingly ambitious computation mathematically meaningful.

AIの発展によって，科学研究で扱える問題の規模や複雑さは大きく変わろうとしています．私はこの時代を見据え，大規模・マルチスケール系における未解像ダイナミクスの安定縮約と，有限要素法を中心とする数値計算の数学的保証を研究の二つの柱に据えています．より多くの変数や複雑な現象に挑戦することと，得られた計算結果をどこまで信頼できるのか数学的に明らかにすること．この両方を進めたいと考えています．

Core programmes

Two distinct theories.

Two mathematically distinct programmes, chosen for an era in which AI-assisted research can explore ever larger and more complex systems, while mathematical reliability remains essential.

AIによって大きな問題を探索できる可能性が広がる一方で，計算結果の信頼性を数学的に確かめる必要があります．この二つの研究は，その問題に異なる方向から取り組むものです．

Programme I

Mathematical foundations of certified finite element analysis

I investigate when finite element approximations remain mathematically reliable. Starting from computable geometric conditions and anisotropic interpolation, I study how approximation spaces, stability and the preservation of mathematical structure affect the properties that discretisation can retain and the errors it can control.

異方性メッシュの幾何条件や補間理論を出発点に，有限要素離散化が安定性，保存則，単調性などをどの条件で保持できるのかを考えます．さらに，数値解の正しさをどこまで計算可能な誤差評価で保証できるかを追究します．

Programme II

Stable reduction of unresolved dynamics

I investigate what information is lost when unresolved degrees of freedom are eliminated, which effects must be retained, and when the resulting dynamics can be trusted. Memory and delay provide mathematical representations of those effects, but their presence alone does not establish a stable reduction.

未解像自由度を消去したとき，何の情報が失われ，どの影響を残さなければならないのかを問います．記憶や遅れはその解析手段です．最終的には，縮約を許容できる場合，許容できない場合，現時点の情報では判定できない場合を数学的に区別する理論を目指します．

Why these two programmes? — なぜこの二つの研究テーマなのか

I have deliberately chosen problems whose scale and complexity make them difficult to explore by hand. AI-assisted computation may allow us to investigate systems with far more coupled variables, widely separated time scales and richer parameter dependence than before. I want the mathematics to grow alongside that capacity, rather than simply use AI to accelerate familiar calculations.

Unresolved-dynamics theory asks which effects of discarded degrees of freedom must be retained to describe a large system faithfully. Certified finite element analysis, building on my work in anisotropic numerical analysis, asks how to control approximation error and assess the reliability of computed solutions. The first direction confronts the scale of the problem; the second confronts the credibility of its computational answer. Both programmes have their own mathematical questions, but together they respond to the same challenge of AI-era scientific computation.

AI時代を見据えた三つの研究方針

  1. AI時代だからこそ，大規模で複雑な数学的問題に挑戦する． 人間だけでは探索が難しかった高次元系，マルチスケール問題，未解像自由度を含む力学系に取り組みます．
  2. AIの計算能力が向上するほど，研究の可能性も広がるテーマを選ぶ． 単に既存の問題を速く解くのではなく，より大きな系や複雑な構造を扱える研究体系を目指します．
  3. 計算能力がどれほど向上しても，数学的保証は不可欠である． AIを利用して得られた予測，数値解，縮約モデルの信頼性を，誤差評価・安定性・保存構造などによって明らかにします．

これらは，二つの研究テーマを選んだ理由でもあります．未解像ダイナミクスでは，多数の自由度を持つ系において，何を消去し，何を残せば元の現象を記述できるかを問います．数学的保証では，近似・離散化・縮約に伴う誤差や構造の変化を評価し，どこまで計算結果を信頼できるかを問います．一方では問題の大きさに挑戦し，他方では計算結果の信頼性を追究する．それぞれ独立した数学的課題を持ちながら，AI時代の科学計算を支える二つの方向だと考えています．

AI4Math · Mathematical Assurance

Mathematics for trustworthy scientific AI

The rapid development of AI is part of my motivation for choosing research problems at a larger scale. I want AI to expand the range of systems and phenomena we can explore, while mathematics supplies the stability analysis, error bounds and structural understanding needed to interpret the results. This is why I pursue both unresolved-dynamics reduction and certified numerical analysis, rather than viewing AI4Math solely as automated theorem proving.

learning / reduction / discretisation→information loss→mathematical certificate→reliable scientific prediction

The long-term goal is to connect certified PDE computation, stable reduction of unresolved dynamics, structure-preserving learning and computable error control into scientific AI systems that can assess and improve the reliability of their own numerical predictions.

AI4MathMathematical assuranceCertified PDE computationStable reductionStructure-preserving learningSelf-correcting computation

Guiding principle: not only AI for mathematics, but mathematics for trustworthy AI.

AI4Math · Human-guided discovery

From large-scale exploration to mathematical guarantees.

A research methodology for unresolved dynamics and multiscale systems, where numerical exploration is directed by mathematical questions and followed by independent analysis.

I am exploring a research cycle in which AI helps design and run structured computational experiments, organise the resulting data and search for counterexamples. Human mathematical judgement then identifies meaningful phenomena, formulates hypotheses and directs the next stage of analysis. The cycle is intended to support mathematical discovery, not to replace proof or verification.

human: formulate the question→AI-assisted computation→human: interpret the data→proof and certification→next question
01

Formulate

Specify the PDE or dynamical system, the resolved observables, assumptions, norms and quantities to be tested.

02

Explore

Use AI-assisted coding and analysis to vary unresolved dimensions, scale separation, coefficients and initial data. Record reproducible outputs.

03

Interpret

Examine trends and failures critically; identify candidate invariants, thresholds, memory effects and counterexamples.

04

Certify

Separate model-reduction and discretisation errors, audit arguments, and seek rigorous bounds or conditions that explain the observations.

An initial test case is a coupled resolved–unresolved linear evolution system. Eliminating the unresolved variables produces a memory term exactly; numerical experiments can then probe which stability and error estimates remain uniform as the unresolved dimension or time-scale separation changes. Observed patterns are conjectures, not established theorems, until the mathematical assumptions and proof have been verified.

This approach is designed to benefit from advances in computational capability while retaining the central responsibility of mathematical research: deciding what a result means, what has actually been established and what should be studied next. It complements the Education & Learning principle of learning to verify and formulate one's own questions.

Research architecture · Programme I

From geometric analysis to mathematical assurance.

The established analytical foundation connects element geometry, affine and Piola transformations, interpolation, stability, consistency and PDE error estimates. The same framework includes standard isotropic meshes while making anisotropic geometric deterioration explicit. Current research asks how this foundation can be extended to structural properties and computable certificates.

Programme II · Open mathematical questions

Stable reduction theory for unresolved dynamics

Eliminating unresolved variables from evolution equations need not remove their dynamical influence. Memory, delay, history forcing and internal-state representations can describe retained effects, but such representations alone do not guarantee stability or fidelity to the full system.

Information lossReduction criteriaPositive-type memoryGraph-space well-posednessMeasure-valued delayKernel stability

The proposed research route is to quantify the influence of eliminated states and develop admissibility criteria; the final certification step is an aim rather than an established theorem.

Full dynamics→Eliminate unresolved states→Quantify information loss→Test admissibility→Certify if justified

I seek criteria that distinguish admissible, inadmissible and presently uncertifiable reductions. Current mathematical tools include operator-valued memory, coercivity and stability bounds. Further questions concern the derivation of closure operators, full-versus-reduced energy structures, and whether reduction and discretisation commute.
