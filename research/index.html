Research lines

G / R / A / V

Four lines describe how individual projects move through geometry, reliable simulation, automation and verification.

G

Geometry & Anisotropic FEM Theory

Computable geometric parameters, semi-regular meshes, anisotropic interpolation, nonconforming and mixed elements, discrete functional inequalities and curved-domain geometry.

R

Reliable PDE Simulation & Reduction

Certified and goal-oriented simulation, time- and history-dependent PDEs, pressure-robust flow methods, graph-space analysis and stable unresolved-dynamics reduction.

A

Approximation, Automation & Learning

Adaptive FEM, mesh and method selection, data-informed kernel identification, structure-preserving approximation and comparisons with PINNs, Deep Ritz and meshless methods.

V

Verification, Formal Reasoning & AI4Math

Computable certificates, equilibrated fluxes, formalisation-ready assumptions and estimates, AI-assisted reasoning and reproducible mathematical structure.

Research summary

Foundations already developed.

A compact map of the main results and themes developed across the finite-element programme.

01

Geometric conditions and maximum-angle theory

New geometric parameters and their relation to classical angle conditions for simplices.

02

Unified anisotropic interpolation theory

Directional and scaling-based error estimates beyond standard shape-regular settings.

03

Nonconforming and mixed finite elements

CR and RT analysis on anisotropic meshes, including stability, consistency and conservation structure.

04

Nitsche and DG methods

Weak boundary imposition and over-penalised interior-penalty methods under relaxed geometric conditions.

05

Navier–Stokes and pressure robustness

Modified CR schemes and robust discretisations for incompressible flow.

06

Discrete Sobolev and Korn inequalities

Functional-analytic foundations needed for nonconforming methods on semi-regular meshes.

07

Certified output and equilibrated fluxes

CR–RT certification, hypercircle-type estimates and goal-oriented error control.

08

Memory and history-dependent evolution equations

Positive-type memory, graph-space well-posedness, measure-valued delays and kernel stability.

Emerging focus

Stable reduction theory for unresolved dynamics

In multiscale evolution equations, unresolved or latent variables may be removed from the resolved state without their influence disappearing. The resulting effect can survive as memory, delay, history forcing, effective dissipation or an internal-state representation.

Positive-type memoryGraph-space well-posednessMeasure-valued delayKernel stabilityOperator-valued memory
unresolved dynamics→memory representation→stable reduction→structure-preserving discretisation

The next stage is to derive memory and closure operators directly from unresolved generators, compare the energy structure of full and reduced systems, and analyse whether reduction and discretisation commute.
