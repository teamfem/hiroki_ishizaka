Research principle

What happens when assumptions are relaxed?

A recurring theme is to determine which assumptions are essential, which can be weakened, and what mathematical or computational phenomena appear when standard conditions are removed.

Four research lines

G / R / A / V.

The four lines describe the flow of the research rather than four isolated subjects.

G

Geometry & Anisotropic FEM Theory

Flatness-type parameters, semi-regular meshes, two-step affine transformations, anisotropic interpolation, nonconforming and mixed methods, and discrete Sobolev/Korn inequalities.

R

Reliable & Goal-Oriented PDE Simulation

Verified simulation, equilibrated fluxes, hypercircle and goal-oriented estimates, pressure-robust flow discretisations, and time- or history-dependent PDEs.

A

Automation, Learning & New Numerical Methods

Adaptive FEM, automated mesh and scheme selection, connections with PINNs, Deep Ritz and meshless methods, and data-informed identification of reduced structures.

V

Verification, Formal Reasoning & AI4Math

Computable certificates, explicit assumptions and constants, proof-oriented organisation of FEM theory, and possible future connections with formalised mathematics.

Research summary

Eight strands of completed and current work.

These strands reproduce the main research summary already present on the current Research page, but in a more navigable form.

01 · Geometry

New geometric conditions and maximum-angle theory

Geometric parameters based on element volume and edge lengths, including conditions equivalent to classical maximum-angle restrictions.

02 · Interpolation

Unified anisotropic interpolation analysis

Two-step affine transformations and directional scaling for interpolation estimates beyond conventional shape-regular meshes.

03 · Nonconforming / mixed

CR and Raviart–Thomas analysis

Anisotropic error analysis for nonconforming and mixed finite elements, with consistency, conservation and pressure robustness in view.

04 · Weak imposition / DG

Nitsche and interior-penalty methods

Stable formulations under semi-regular and anisotropic mesh conditions, including weakly over-penalised approaches.

05 · Flow

Navier–Stokes and pressure robustness

Discretisations designed to control nonlinear effects and pressure contamination, with an eye to stable time-dependent extensions.

06 · Foundations

Discrete Sobolev and Poincaré inequalities

Discrete functional inequalities under anisotropic or semi-regular mesh assumptions as a theoretical basis for nonconforming analysis.

07 · Certification

Certified output and equilibrated-flux methods

CR–RT flux reconstruction, hypercircle-type estimates and goal-oriented control for explicit numerical error certificates.

08 · Memory / reduction

History-dependent PDEs and unresolved dynamics

Positive-type memory, degenerate instantaneous diffusion, measure-valued delays and kernel stability as analytical ingredients for stable reduction.

Emerging focus · R / A / V

Stable reduction theory for unresolved dynamics

In multiscale evolution equations, removing fine degrees of freedom or latent variables does not imply that their influence disappears. Their effect can remain in resolved dynamics through operator-valued memory, delay, history forcing, effective dissipation or internal states.

Current analytical ingredients include positive-type memory, graph-space well-posedness for degenerate instantaneous diffusion, weighted well-posedness for measure-valued delays, and stability under kernel perturbation, atomic approximation and delay concentration.

  • CR–RT equilibrated fluxes and hypercircle-type estimates.
