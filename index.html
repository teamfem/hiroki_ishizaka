Two research programmes

The website is organised around two mathematical programmes rather than a long list of individual topics.

Programme I · Finite element analysis

Certified finite element analysis under computable geometric conditions

I study how mesh geometry controls approximation, stability, monotonicity, and computable error certification. A central theme is to obtain quantitative results on anisotropic meshes without hiding geometric deterioration inside unspecified constants.

anisotropic FEMmesh geometryCR / RTMorleyNitsche / DGStokes / Navier–Stokesequilibrated flux
geometry → approximation → stability → certificate
Programme II · Evolution equations

Stable reduction theory for unresolved dynamics

I study what mathematical structure survives when latent or unresolved variables are eliminated from evolution equations. The resulting reduced dynamics may contain memory, delay, or hidden internal states; the goal is to identify stable state spaces, sharp stability boundaries, and approximation principles that remain certifiable. The long-term aim is reliable prediction and control in systems whose dynamically relevant states are only partially resolved.

About

Numerical analysis across geometry, dynamics, and certification

I am an independent researcher in numerical analysis. My work spans finite element analysis, partial differential equations, and mathematical certification, with a particular interest in mathematical structures that computation or model reduction can leave unseen.

Current emphasis: anisotropic and nonconforming FEM, computable geometric conditions, CR–RT structures, certified error control, and stable reduction of unresolved dynamics.
