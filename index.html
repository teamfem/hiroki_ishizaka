Research mission

Mathematics makes the invisible visible. In my research, the “invisible” includes discretisation error, the influence of mesh geometry, unresolved scales, latent memory, and the limits of numerical prediction. I study the mathematics needed to make these hidden effects visible, quantifiable, and controllable, so that numerical prediction becomes stable, accurate, and certifiable. My work currently develops two complementary research programmes:

  1. Certified finite element analysis under computable geometric conditions
    計算可能な幾何条件に基づく，保証付き有限要素解析
  2. Stable reduction theory for unresolved dynamics
    未解像ダイナミクスの安定縮約理論

The first programme asks how mesh geometry, anisotropy, nonconformity, flux reconstruction, and goal-oriented error estimation determine the reliability of finite element computation. The second asks what remains after fine scales, latent states, or unobserved variables are eliminated, and how the resulting memory, delay, nonlocal closure, and internal states can be approximated without destroying stability. Their common objective is trustworthy simulation: not only producing a numerical prediction, but also explaining its stability, error, unresolved influence, and range of validity.

Programme I — Certified finite element analysis

A central question in my finite element research is: how does the geometry of a computational mesh control approximation, stability, and certification? I study computable geometric conditions, including semi-regularity and flatness-type parameters, and use them to develop anisotropic error estimates for nonstandard finite elements such as Crouzeix–Raviart, Morley, discontinuous Galerkin, Nitsche, and mixed methods.

The long-term objective is to connect

\[ \text{scheme design} \;\times\; \text{mesh geometry} \;\times\; \text{error estimation} \;\times\; \text{verification} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{certified computation}. \]

This includes discrete Sobolev and Korn inequalities, equilibrated fluxes, hypercircle-type estimates, dual-weighted residual methods, pressure-robust flow discretisations, and computable certificates for quantities of interest.

Programme II — Stable reduction theory for unresolved dynamics

When unresolved degrees of freedom are removed from an evolution equation, their influence does not generally disappear. It may remain as operator-valued memory, delayed or history-dependent forcing, spatially nonlocal closure, internal states, or stochastic fluctuation. The purpose of stable reduction theory is to construct and analyse reduced models that retain relevant stability, dissipation, causality, and singular-limit structure of the full dynamics.

\[ \text{full dynamics} \longrightarrow \text{resolved / unresolved decomposition} \longrightarrow \text{memory or nonlocal closure} \longrightarrow \text{stable reduced dynamics} \longrightarrow \text{certified computation}. \]

Current work develops the analytical core of this programme through graph-space well-posedness, positive-type and measure-valued memory, kernel stability, internal-variable representations, and singular limits such as memory concentration and vanishing instantaneous coercivity. The next stage is to derive and approximate such kernels and closure operators directly from unresolved generators, with applications to convection-dominated diffusion, incompressible flow, multiscale systems, and data-informed reduced models.

Central questions include:

  • Which state space, energy, and dissipation mechanism survive reduction?
  • How do projection, kernel, closure, and unresolved-initial-state errors propagate to the resolved solution?
  • Do reduction, discretisation, homogenisation, and singular limits commute?
  • How can memory and internal states be approximated while preserving positivity, causality, and stability?

Research architecture — G / R / A / V

The two programmes are developed through four interacting research lines:

  • G — Geometry & Anisotropic FEM Theory
    Computable geometric parameters, semi-regular meshes, anisotropic interpolation, nonconforming and mixed elements, discrete functional inequalities, and curved-domain geometry.
  • R — Reliable PDE Simulation & Reduction
    Time-dependent and history-dependent PDEs, graph-space well-posedness, stable reduction of unresolved dynamics, pressure-robust Stokes/Navier–Stokes methods, and verified or goal-oriented error control.
  • A — Approximation, Automation & Learning
    Adaptive FEM, mesh and method selection, structure-preserving approximation of memory and internal states, data-informed kernel identification, and connections with PINNs, Deep Ritz, and meshless methods.
  • V — Verification, Formal Reasoning & AI4Math
    Computable certificates, equilibrated-flux and goal-oriented bounds, reproducible mathematical structure, AI-assisted reasoning, and future formalisation.

Why trustworthy simulation?

Digital twins, scientific machine learning, and data-informed models increasingly connect simulation with practical decisions. In such settings, a visually plausible prediction is not enough: one must quantify approximation error, unresolved effects, extrapolation risk, conservation defects, and the resolution needed for a target quantity. My aim is to develop the mathematical reliability layer linking PDE analysis, numerical discretisation, model reduction, and verification.

Relevant broader contexts include Verification, Validation and Uncertainty Quantification (ASME VVUQ), physics-informed learning and its failure modes (PINN failure modes), and urban or disaster-oriented digital-twin simulation (Kobe digital-twin project).

Near-future directions

  • Stable reduction of unresolved dynamics: derive memory and nonlocal closures from unresolved generators and establish resolution- and singular-limit stability.
  • Structure-preserving memory discretisation: combine finite internal states, convolution quadrature, and anisotropic CR–RT approximation.
  • Certified error control for decision-making: provide computable error radii and certificates for quantities of interest.
  • Reliability layers for surrogates and physics-ML: detect boundary-layer, extrapolation, conservation, and under-resolution failures.
  • AI4Math-ready numerical analysis: organise geometric conditions, stability mechanisms, and error certificates in verifiable and reusable forms.
