Geometric Conditions in FEM

From angle conditions
to computable geometry.

Finite-element accuracy depends not only on approximation spaces and weak formulations, but also on the geometry of the mesh family. This page follows the development from classical angle conditions to anisotropic interpolation and computable semi-regular geometric parameters.

1943CourantAn early finite-element prototype and the beginning of the broader historical setting.
1957–1976Angle conditionsSynge, Zlámal and Babuška–Aziz establish the central role of element geometry.
1991–1999Semiregularity & anisotropyKřížek, Apel and related work expand the geometric language beyond shape regularity.
2021–2024Computable parametersFlatness / semi-regular formulations and anisotropic CR, RT and Morley analysis.
Historical map

Three questions organise the story.

The full historical text from the previous page remains available below. The cards provide a compact entrance rather than replacing the detailed discussion.

I · admissible shapes

Which element shapes can a mesh family safely contain?

Maximum-angle, minimum-angle, shape-regular and semi-regular conditions answer different versions of this question.

II · anisotropy

How can long, thin elements be analysed without hiding degeneration?

Anisotropic estimates separate mesh size, direction and shape deterioration instead of treating every thin element as equally bad.

III · computability

Can the geometry entering error constants be measured directly?

Computable geometric parameters are useful when theory is meant to connect to mesh quality, certification and adaptivity.
